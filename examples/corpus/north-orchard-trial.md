# North Orchard Drip Irrigation Trial

The North Orchard trial compared two watering schedules for the 2024 apple
block. Mira Chen designed the trial after the previous season showed uneven
fruit size along the eastern rows. The eastern rows sit on a shallow gravel
layer, so water drains faster there than in the western rows.

Two zones were instrumented. Zone A used the existing drip line schedule of
forty minutes every second morning. Zone B used a soil-moisture trigger that
started irrigation whenever the probe at twenty centimetres fell below 18
percent volumetric water content. Both zones received the same emitter type and
the same filter maintenance.

The trial ran from 12 May to 30 September. Data loggers recorded soil moisture
every fifteen minutes. Mira Chen reviewed the logs each Friday and flagged any
probe that drifted by more than two percentage points against a handheld
reading. Two probes were replaced in July: one in Zone A row 8 and one in Zone B
row 3.

Zone B used about 19 percent less water over the season. Fruit size was
similar between zones, but Zone B had fewer split apples after the August
rain. The result supported keeping the moisture trigger for the eastern rows
and returning the western rows to the fixed schedule, where the probes were
slower to respond.

Open questions remain. The trial did not measure root depth, and the gravel
layer may make the twenty-centimetre probe unrepresentative during dry spells.
A follow-up should add a deeper probe in Zone B and run for at least two
seasons before changing the whole block.
